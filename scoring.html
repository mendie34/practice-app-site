How scoring works

Strokes gained — and now, Par-style scoring too

Every drill already scores in strokes gained. Distance Control and Short Game now also score Eagle, Birdie, Par, Bogey and Double — the same language as a real round of golf.

Strokes gained

Every shot is compared to a fixed baseline — from 30-handicap to PGA Tour — so a putt, a wedge shot and a drive can all be measured on the same scale. This is what every trend chart and Analysis tab in the app is built on.

Par-style scoring

Each shot is scored Eagle, Birdie, Par, Bogey or Double based on how close it finishes — to your target on the range, to the hole in the short game — and rolls up into a running “round to par,” just like a real card.

Where the numbers come from

The bands are not guesses — here is how each one was built.

Scaled by ability

All 8 baseline tiers — Tour, Scratch, and 5 through 30 handicap — have their own bands, so “Par” means a realistic result at your level, not a tour pro’s.

Lie-specific for Short Game

Fairway, rough and bunker each get their own thresholds at every tier — bunker is consistently the hardest lie in every proximity dataset checked, so one universal band would score it unfairly.

Built from real proximity data

Short Game’s bands synthesize figures from MyGolfSpy, Shot Scope, World Of Short Game, The Left Rough and Arccos — real measured proximity-to-hole, not invented numbers.
